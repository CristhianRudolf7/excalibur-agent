# Scholarly search

Use this skill for peer-reviewed and biomedical literature beyond arXiv. Open indexes give citation graphs and metadata that rank influence and find the primary source.

## When to use

- Medical, biological, or cross-disciplinary questions.
- Tracing citation chains to primary evidence.
- Needing peer-reviewed backing for a claim.

## Procedure

1. Search the open indexes with precise terms; filter by date and type.
2. Follow citation graphs toward the primary studies.
3. Prefer peer-reviewed and replicated findings; mark preprints as such.
4. Extract methods and effect sizes, not conclusions alone.
5. Record identifiers (DOI, PMID) with every citation.

## Output

Literature findings with identifiers and evidence grades.

## Pitfalls

- Review articles summarize; primary studies decide.
- Biomedical claims need stronger sourcing; say when it is missing.
